## Winner

D05 — 25/25, the only perfect score.
"Do not mourn me. Mourn the Wi-Fi password. It dies with me." (59 chars)

Why it wins: it is first-person dying speech (criterion 1, strongest of all
drafts — the dead man himself speaks, not a narrator); the laugh is real and
modern (everyone in the field has fought over a Wi-Fi password); the twist
lands on the last four words — the thing mourned is not the man but the
password; three short imperative sentences read exactly like carved stone;
and it is the most quotable line here — repeatable to a friend in one breath.
Runner-ups D03/D14/D18/D23 all score 24/25: each loses one point somewhere
(D03's grammar joke is slightly niche, D14's "oops" is a touch thin on
memorability, D18 needs the name Bob to land, D23's twist is a half-step
weaker). D05 loses nothing.

Rejected on purpose: the famous Spike Milligan epitaph "I told you I was
ill" was NOT used — it is a known published epitaph and could match a
house text.
